On what legal ground do we process your data?

As Article 10 of Law No. 6698 (the KVKK) requires, the legal ground of each processing is written separately. Where a processing does not rest on your explicit consent, you do not need to give consent; showing that you have read this text is enough.

  • Account (name, e-mail, password hash, devices, document records, sign-in events): the setting up and performance of a contract, to set up and provide the account (art. 5/2-c).
  • Sharing, Live and Classes in earlier versions: the performance of a contract, to provide the feature you asked for (art. 5/2-c).
  • Experience records: FLOVEY’s legitimate interest in making eynote run without stalls and errors (art. 5/2-f); this text is given under the duty to inform (art. 10). It is not explicit consent; the records are kept for every account and you can object.
  • eynote AI: your explicit consent (art. 5/1). With GPT-6 Luna chosen your data is processed by OpenAI, with Claude Haiku 5.5 chosen by Anthropic, in the United States of America; every request is a transfer abroad. This transfer rests on the explicit consent you give after the possible risks are told to you (art. 9). The consent for OpenAI is given on eynote AI’s consent card, the consent for Anthropic on Claude Haiku 5.5’s separate consent card; giving one does not cover the other. FLOVEY plans to move both transfers to standard contracts (art. 9/4), to be made separately with OpenAI and with Anthropic.
  • Student groups: the performance of a contract (art. 5/2-c); for those under 18, also the explicit consent of a parent or guardian.

If you use Sharing, Live and groups

If you share with a link or open a live session, the encrypted form of the document is uploaded to the eynote server. The content is encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM before it is uploaded. The key does not go to the eynote server in the clear; it stays in the part of the link after the # sign. So the eynote server cannot open encrypted shared and live-session content. You sign in with an eynote account to make a link, to open or join a live session and to use groups; no account is needed to open a share link.

  • Share link: The encrypted document stays on the eynote server for the time you choose (at most 365 days). Everyone who gets the link can open the document. Unencrypted, the eynote server sees only your account, the size and hash of the encrypted file, and the creation and expiry times; the title is uploaded encrypted too. If you stop the share in My Library › Shared, the link stops working and the encrypted copy is deleted from the eynote server (if the delete request does not arrive, within about a day at the latest). When the time runs out the link stops working too, and the encrypted copy is cleared from the eynote server. Copies already downloaded to the devices of those who opened the link are not deleted.
  • Live session: The document’s snapshot, the session title and every change are uploaded encrypted. The eynote server keeps the changes after the last snapshot up to at most 1000 messages or 8 MiB. Unencrypted, it sees the accounts that opened and joined the session (the names are shown to everyone in the session), the time of joining, which account each change came from and its approximate size, and the session’s opening and last-activity times. If you end the session, the encrypted data is deleted 7 days after the end; if you do not, 7 days after the last activity. Copies already downloaded to participants’ devices are not deleted. The session’s working copy is not written to your account’s document list.
  • Groups:

    Your group content (notes, titles, comments and messages, set and task names, offers made to the leader) is encrypted on your device; in normal operation the eynote server does not read this content. However, in today’s version a server acting in bad faith or taken over could step into the key distribution between devices, take a group’s keys and so read that group’s content, including the content of earlier periods. We are redesigning the key distribution to reduce this risk.

    Unencrypted, the eynote server sees the group’s name; the members’ names, roles (group leader, helper, member), joining times and whether they can invite; join requests and their times; who made an invite code, until when it is valid and whether it was used; who added and edited a note and when, the notes’ version numbers and encrypted sizes; who opened a set; whom a task was given to, its due date and who completed it and when; whom comments and messages came from, their times and lengths; who sent an offer to the leader, for which note and against which version of the note, when it was sent, the offer’s encrypted size and state, when the leader decided or when the offer was withdrawn, whether a reason was written for a decline and the reason’s encrypted size, and which version of the note an approved offer is; in which group and for which note or record, by whom, when and from which IP address the group’s actions were done (setting up, editing, archiving and handing over the group, invites, joining and membership changes, sharing, updating and deleting notes and their permissions, the confirmation that a note reached a device, set, task and comment actions, sending, withdrawing, approving, declining and deleting offers to the leader, device key actions) (it keeps these records for a year); the group’s settings and leadership handover requests; the devices’ public keys for the group, signed records and encrypted key envelopes.
  • Offer to leader: What you write yourself on a note shared with a group stays with you only. If you offer some of it to the group leader, the offer is encrypted on your device with a key only you and the group leader can open, and uploaded; the group’s other members and the eynote server cannot see its content. If the leader approves, what you wrote becomes the note’s new version and everyone in the group sees it; a reason the leader may write when declining is encrypted too. An offer not decided becomes void after 30 days; the record of an offer that was decided or ran out is deleted from the eynote server after 90 days.

This information is processed to run share links, live sessions and groups and to look into abuse. Unless you use these features, the information in this section does not arise on the eynote server. If you report a person or content from the app, the description you write goes unencrypted to the eynote support team for review. If you add a copy of the note or comment to the report, the copy is encrypted on your device so that only FLOVEY’s review team can open it; the team can open and read it, and it may contain what others wrote. If you use the Browser

The Browser is the site panel that opens beside the page inside eynote (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or an address you type). You use the sites with your own accounts; eynote does not reach those sites’ accounts.

  • Connection: Sites are opened straight from this device; nothing goes through eynote’s server. The eynote server only says whether the Browser is open on your account (Plus, Premium, trial, class or group membership); it does not know which site you open.
  • Search: Words you type into the address bar that are not an address are searched on Google; the search goes straight from this device to Google, not through eynote’s server. Google’s own terms apply. Nothing is sent while you type (there are no suggestions or preloading).
  • eynote does not see inside the site: Only when you take a snapshot does the site’s picture at that moment come to eynote; the picture frozen for the snapshot stays only in memory, is not kept and is not sent anywhere. If you drag a snapshot from your page onto the site or choose “Paste into the site”, only that picture is put on the clipboard and pasted into the site’s text field; the document’s name or anything else does not go to the site.
  • What stays on your device: Your sign-ins on sites, cookies and site data stay on this device, in the Browser’s own part; they are separate from eynote’s documents, do not go into the eynote backup and are not synced. They are not deleted when a plan or trial ends. Settings › Privacy › Browser sign-ins and site data › Delete… deletes all of them from this device; your accounts on the sites are not deleted. The device remembers only the last address you opened and the panel’s width.
  • Permissions: If a site asks for the microphone or the camera, eynote asks you; the permission holds for that site for this session. Other requests such as notifications and location are refused without asking. Downloads are asked about first and saved to Downloads.
  • Limits: For Browser connections that go through the proxy, eynote sends the name to the system’s resolver, checks every address that comes back against the common address policy and connects only to the checked address. This policy refuses local, private, reserved, documentation, benchmarking and globally unreachable addresses; it also blocks the whole 192.0.0/24 and 192.88.99/24 ranges, ORCHID and Teredo. A NAT64 or 6to4 address is allowed if the IPv4 address inside it is allowed. Site names reach the device’s configured DNS resolver. On Android, WebRTC does not go through this check: a site can send STUN packets to local addresses and ports, and can set up a data connection only with a peer that takes part in it; a site you give microphone or camera permission can learn the device’s local addresses. On Android, after the Browser has been used, the check and the proxy stay until the app’s process ends, even when the views and the Activity close; the app’s own WebView traffic goes through the same policy too, and the local eynote server address is blocked. On Android 10 and later, another app that finds the proxy’s address and port can use it, until the eynote process ends, to reach addresses the policy allows. Open tunnels are limited by idle and closing times; these times do not remove the check. On every platform a site’s certificate is checked after the connection to its checked address is made.

Experience records

The app keeps and sends usage summaries for every eynote account, to understand stalls and errors. The records are tied to this device, not to an account: once they have started on this device they go on even if you sign out or sign in with another account (the plan type is then sent as “signed out”); to stop them, you object. Their legal ground is not explicit consent but legitimate interest (KVKK art. 5/2-f); this text is given under the duty to inform (art. 10). When you open an account or sign in for the first time, you tick the line “I have read that my records are kept, and I agree” in this text; this line shows that you read the text and is not consent. As proof, your account id, the text’s version and the time are kept on the eynote server (no installation id or device information is written to this record). Those who already had an account are shown this text once after the update. You can choose “Object to the records / turn off” in Settings › Privacy at any time (art. 11): the records stop and are deleted; you can read what was sent and delete it.

  • What is sent: Stall times while writing, zooming and opening; saving times and saving errors; the error type and which file of the app it happened in (no error message); counts of stalls such as repeated taps and repeated undos; how many times each tool and feature was used; the app version, platform and operating system, language, theme, plan type, screen type, screen density, number of processor cores, memory and whether there is a pen.
  • What is not sent: Note, drawing and document content, document names, search words, eynote AI messages, name, e-mail and account id. The eynote server does not write the IP address to these records; rate-limit counters are kept in memory only.
  • Identity: A random id on the device; it is not matched to your account and is renewed when you object. A deletion key goes with the first sending; the eynote server keeps only its hash.
  • Keeping: Records that could not be sent wait on the device for at most 14 days; sending happens at most once every 15 minutes. On the eynote server, raw session summaries and daily shares per installation are kept 90 days; then they are added to anonymous daily totals and deleted. Anonymous totals are kept for 2 calendar years. Database backups are kept for at most 30 days.
  • Deletion: When you object to the records or tap Delete this device’s records, the queue on the device and your raw records on the eynote server are deleted; with no connection, the delete request is tried again when you are connected. Anonymous daily totals are not deleted, because they cannot be matched to anyone.

Text and handwriting recognition

The text of scanned pages and pictures and your handwriting are recognised on your device; the pages’ images or what you write are not sent to a server for this. On the computer, the recognition model that comes with the app and Windows’ own handwriting recogniser are used.

On Android, recognition is done on the device with Google’s ML Kit library. The handwriting recognition model is downloaded from Google once, on first use, if you agree. According to Google, ML Kit may send Google measurements about the library’s use and performance (for example the device model, the Android and app versions, how long recognition took); these measurements do not carry the document’s content, image or the recognised text, and they are subject to Google’s own privacy terms. On Android, Google Play services also downloads the text-recognition component from Google the first time it is used; this download carries no document content.

Advertising, tracking and screenshots

The app has no advertising, data-selling or tracking SDK (the usage measurements the text recognition library on Android may send, and the experience records kept for every account, are described above). When you tap the Android Quick Settings tile, only the newest screenshot is read. On the desktop, if you turn on screenshot suggestions, new pictures in the folder you chose are suggested; you tap the card to add one. On iPhone and iPad, the picture you give to Shortcuts is processed.

Desktop updates

The eynote app on a computer asks the eynote site by itself, after it opens and every six hours, whether there is a new version. If there is, it is shown to you; if you choose to download it, the installer comes from GitHub. Because the site and the installers are kept on GitHub (GitHub, Inc., United States of America), GitHub sees your IP address at that time; your eynote account, name or documents are not sent. You can turn this check off with the Settings › Help › App update › Check for updates automatically switch; while it is off, the app asks only when you tap Check for updates.

Your rights under the KVKK

Under Article 11 of Law No. 6698, by applying to the data controller you can use these rights:

  • to learn whether your personal data is processed;
  • if it is, to ask for information about it;
  • to learn the purpose of the processing and whether it is used for that purpose;
  • to know the third parties in Turkey or abroad it is transferred to;
  • to ask for it to be corrected if it is processed incompletely or wrongly;
  • to ask for it to be erased or destroyed under the conditions of Article 7 of the Law;
  • to ask for this correction, erasure or destruction to be told to the third parties your data was transferred to;
  • to object to a result against you that comes from the processed data being analysed only by automated systems;
  • to ask for compensation if you suffer damage because your personal data was processed against the Law.

You can also find these rights explained on the page of the Personal Data Protection Authority (in Turkish).

How do you use them? Objecting to experience records: Settings › Privacy › Object to the records / turn off. Withdrawing eynote AI’s consent: Settings › eynote AI › Withdraw consent. Information, access, correction, deletion and other requests: the contact e-mail above; your application is answered within thirty days at the latest.

To apply, reach the data controller at the contact address above. You can delete the documents you keep on your device from the app and, if you connected to Drive, manage their copies there from your Google Drive account too.

Deleting your account

From the app: you can delete your eynote account at once with My account › Account › Delete account…. You confirm again that the account is yours with your password or with the Google account linked to it. This cannot be undone.

If you cannot reach the app: you can send your account deletion request to the contact e-mail above; your application is answered within thirty days at the latest.

  • What is deleted: your account and profile, your plan, your sessions and device list, your document list, your sign-in events (failed attempts with that e-mail too), your share links (they stop opening), the live sessions you opened, your group memberships, the notes you shared with groups and the comments you wrote, your documents in server storage, your eynote AI consents, usage records and the pictures you added to chats, your reading-mode translations, your class records from earlier versions. If a group you lead has a helper who can be leader, the group passes to them; otherwise the group closes and its records are deleted after 30 days. You are signed out of the account on your other devices.
  • What stays for a while: security records (what you did and your IP address; your name is removed from these records) 365 days; database backups at most 30 days; reports you made or that were made about you, without the description and not tied to your name, at most 365 days; the record of a gift code you used, not tied to your account. Experience records are not tied to your account: raw records stay for at most 90 days; before deleting your account you can delete them at once with Settings › Privacy › Delete this device’s records. Monthly eynote AI totals with no link to an account and anonymous daily experience totals stay, because they cannot be matched to anyone.
  • What is not deleted: the documents and notes on this device and your other devices, and the copies in your own Google Drive. You delete these yourself, from the app or from Google Drive.
